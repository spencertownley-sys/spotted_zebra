**Quick answer:** For live music in Georgetown, Seattle, start on **Airport Way South**. **Jules Maes Saloon**, one of Seattle's oldest bars, hosts live bands, and **9 LB Hammer** down the street is a longtime music bar. Just west, **Slim's Last Chance** is a rowdy roadhouse with live music. Spotted Zebra plays Jules Maes on **Saturday, November 28** for [Second Serving Saturday](/blog/second-serving-saturday-jules-maes-seattle/).

Georgetown is Seattle's old industrial neighborhood, wedged between Boeing Field and the Duwamish, and it has quietly become one of the best places in the city to catch a band in a bar that still feels like a bar. Here's where to go.

## Jules Maes Saloon

**5919 Airport Way S.** One of Seattle's oldest bars, dating to the late 1800s, and a room that fills up fast when a band is on. We've played here more than once, and it's always one of our favorite nights. Our next show here is **Second Serving Saturday on November 28**, with Pink-182 and Lark vs Owl. ## 9 LB Hammer

**Airport Way S.** A longtime Georgetown bar a couple of blocks from Jules Maes, and one of the neighborhood's go-to spots for live music. It's an easy one to pair with a show down the street.

## Slim's Last Chance

**1st Ave S, on the west side of Georgetown.** A rowdy roadhouse with live bands on the bill. It's a different vibe from Airport Way and worth the short trip.

## Make a night of it

- **Start early:** Georgetown has plenty of breweries and restaurants along Airport Way, so dinner and a drink before the music is easy.
- **Bar-hop:** Jules Maes and 9 LB Hammer are close enough to walk between sets.
- **Check before you go:** music nights change, so look at each bar's site or socials for that week's lineup.

## Why Georgetown works for live music

The neighborhood's mix of old brick buildings, industrial spaces and late-night bars means small rooms with real character, the kind of places where you're ten feet from the band. It's also just south of downtown, so it's an easy trip from most of Seattle.
