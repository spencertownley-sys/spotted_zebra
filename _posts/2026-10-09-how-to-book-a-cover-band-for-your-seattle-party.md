A live band turns a party into a *night*. But if you've never booked one before, the logistics can feel like a mystery. Here's the checklist we wish every host had. It's useful whether you hire us or someone else.

## 1. Lock the date and the timeline first

Bands book up, especially on summer weekends and around the holidays in the Seattle area. Once you have a date, sketch a rough timeline:

- When guests arrive
- When you want the music to start (and stop)
- Any moments that need a pause: toasts, cake, speeches, a first dance

Most bands play in **sets** with short breaks in between. Ask how many sets they play and how long each one runs, then fit them around your timeline.

## 2. Measure the space

A full band with drums needs more room than people expect. Before you book, look at:

- **Floor space** for the band, plus room in front for dancing
- **A flat, dry surface**. Outdoor parties in the Pacific Northwest need a rain plan, even in August.
- **Load-in access**: stairs, long walks and tight doorways all matter when you're carrying amps

## 3. Sort out power

Ask the band what they need. Most need a few standard outlets near the playing area, ideally on circuits that aren't shared with the coffee maker and the patio heaters.

## 4. Talk about volume

Live drums are loud. If you're in a neighborhood with noise rules, or the venue has a sound limit, mention it up front. A good band can adjust their setup and volume to the room.

## 5. Share the vibe (and the requests)

Tell the band who's coming and what they love. Is it a crowd of 80s kids? A wedding with three generations on the dance floor? A bar that wants 90s rock all night?

On our site you can [pick songs from our jukebox](/#jukebox) and send them right over, and our [Gig Builder](/#book) lets you tell us which decades you want.

## 6. Get the details in writing

Before the big day, confirm the date, start and end times, address, load-in time, set length, and anything the band needs from you. A short email that everyone agrees on saves a lot of stress.
