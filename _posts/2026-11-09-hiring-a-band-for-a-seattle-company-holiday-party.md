**Quick answer:** For a Seattle company holiday party, book a live band **6–10 weeks ahead** (December weekends go first), plan on **up to three 40-minute sets** for the dancing part of the night, and check the venue's **stage space, power and sound system** early. A cover band that plays recognizable hits from several decades gets the widest range of coworkers onto the dance floor.

A DJ fills the room with sound. A live band fills it with *energy*. When your accounting team is screaming "Livin' on a Prayer" with the folks from engineering, that's the moment people talk about in January. Here's how to plan it.

## When should you book a band for a holiday party?

As early as you can. In the Seattle area, the first three Fridays and Saturdays of December are the most requested dates of the year for corporate events. If you're past mid-November, try:

- **Weeknights** (Tuesday through Thursday), which are often easier to book
- **Early January "New Year" parties**, which are increasingly popular and much easier to schedule

## How should the night run?

For a typical 3–4 hour party:

1. **Arrival and mingling:** a playlist in the background while people grab a drink
2. **Dinner, toasts and awards:** keep the playlist going, then hand the mic to whoever's giving the speeches
3. **Party time:** the band comes on when people are ready to dance

A live band is at its best when it's the main event, not background music, so save it for the part of the night when the room is ready to get loud. Spotted Zebra plays up to three 40-minute sets. Tell the band your run of show so they can save the biggest songs for when the room is full.

## What does the venue need?

Ask your venue (or tell the band) about:

- **Space:** a five-piece band needs roughly a 12 × 16 ft area
- **Power:** a couple of standard outlets on separate circuits near the stage
- **Sound:** whether the venue has a sound system the band can use. Many bands, including us, can bring one, but a venue-provided system makes setup faster and the sound better suited to the room.
- **Load-in:** where the band can unload, and when
- **Volume rules:** some hotels and offices have sound limits. Mention them up front.

## How do you keep it work-appropriate?

Ask for a **clean set**. A professional band will skip or edit songs with explicit lyrics without being reminded. Let them know if there's an awards moment or a CEO toast, so they can time their sets around it.

## Seattle and Eastside venue tips

Holiday parties happen everywhere, from downtown hotel ballrooms and South Lake Union offices to Bellevue event spaces and brewery buyouts in Ballard and Georgetown. Breweries and private bar buyouts are great value. Just double-check that they have room for a band and enough power.
