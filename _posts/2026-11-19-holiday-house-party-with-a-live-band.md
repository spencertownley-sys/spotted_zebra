**Quick answer:** To host a holiday house party with a live band in Seattle, you need about a **12 × 16 ft** space for a five-piece band, **two or three outlets on separate circuits**, and a plan to **wrap up the loud part by 10 PM**, when Seattle's residential quiet hours begin. Book **4–8 weeks ahead** for December weekends. Private parties typically **start around $400**.

A band in the living room turns a holiday party into the one everyone talks about until next December. Here's how to pull it off without blowing a fuse or annoying the neighbors.

## 1. Find the space

A five-piece band needs roughly **12 × 16 ft**. Good spots:

- a **cleared living room** or open-plan kitchen and living area
- a **garage** (warm it up, and put down a rug to tame the echo)
- a **covered patio** with heaters

Put the band at one end and leave the middle open. That's your dance floor.

## 2. Check the power

Bands need a couple of standard outlets near where they set up, ideally on **separate circuits**, so the amps don't share a breaker with the space heater and the slow cooker. If you're not sure, flip breakers with a lamp plugged in to see which outlets are on which circuit.

## 3. Mind the neighbors (and the clock)

Seattle's noise code treats **10 PM** as the start of overnight quiet hours in residential areas: until **7 AM on weekdays** and **9 AM on weekends and holidays**. The easy way to stay on good terms:

- start the music early, around **7 or 8 PM**
- plan the band to finish by **10 PM**, then switch to a playlist
- give the neighbors a heads-up, or better yet, invite them

## 4. Plan the night

| Time | What's happening |
|---|---|
| 6:30 PM | Guests arrive, drinks, playlist |
| 7:30 PM | Band: set one |
| 8:10 PM | Break: food, toasts, ugly-sweater judging |
| 8:30 PM | Band: set two (the big singalongs) |
| 9:10 PM | Break |
| 9:20 PM | Band: final set, ending by 10 PM |

Spotted Zebra plays **up to three 40-minute sets**, which fits this timeline exactly.

## 5. Sort out sound

Many bands can bring a small sound system for a house party. We can, though we're happy to use yours if you have one. Tell the band the room size when you book.

## 6. Pick the songs

Ask your guests what they want to hear. Put the requests in your booking, and look through [our full song list](/jukebox/) for ideas. We can learn holiday songs on request, too.

## What does it cost?

Private parties with a live cover band in Seattle typically **start around $400**, depending on the date, how long you want music and whether the band brings sound. More in our [pricing guide](/blog/how-much-does-a-cover-band-cost-in-seattle/).
