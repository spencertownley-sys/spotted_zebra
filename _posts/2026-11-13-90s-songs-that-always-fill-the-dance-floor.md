**Quick answer:** The 90s songs that fill a dance floor every time we play them are **"Just a Girl" (No Doubt), "Zombie" (The Cranberries), "Flagpole Sitta" (Harvey Danger), "Come Out and Play" (The Offspring), "What's My Age Again?" (blink-182)** and **"My Own Worst Enemy" (Lit)**. Add "Santeria," "Celebrity Skin," "Inside Out" and CAKE's "I Will Survive" and you've got a full set.

These aren't picks from a "best of" list. They're songs from [our actual set](/jukebox/), and they're the ones that pull people out of their seats. Here's why each one works.

## 1. No Doubt, "Just a Girl" (1995)

From *Tragic Kingdom*. That opening riff is instantly recognizable, and the whole room sings the "Oh... I'm just a girl" line. [Watch us play it.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-2fcZ-MbiZc)

## 2. The Cranberries, "Zombie" (1994)

From *No Need to Argue*. It starts heavy and stays heavy, and the "in your head" chorus turns into a full-room shout.

## 3. Harvey Danger, "Flagpole Sitta" (1997)

A Seattle band's one-hit wonder that everyone knows, from *Where Have All the Merrymakers Gone?* Hometown pride plus a chorus built for jumping. [Watch us play it.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kPxmXKnPico)

## 4. The Offspring, "Come Out and Play" (1994)

From *Smash*. Two words: "keep 'em separated." The crowd always gets that part.

## 5. blink-182, "What's My Age Again?" (1999)

From *Enema of the State*. Short, fast and silly in the best way. Nobody is too old for this one.

## 6. Lit, "My Own Worst Enemy" (1999)

From *A Place in the Sun*. The quiet-then-loud chorus is pure late-90s radio, and it never misses. [Watch us play it.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fEm53F-5kG4)

## 7. Sublime, "Santeria" (1996)

From Sublime's self-titled album. It slows the tempo without emptying the floor, which makes it a great breather in the middle of a set.

## 8. Hole, "Celebrity Skin" (1998)

The title track from *Celebrity Skin*. Big guitars and a snarl that wakes up the room.

## 9. Eve 6, "Inside Out" (1998)

From their self-titled debut. Everyone remembers the chorus, and almost nobody expects a band to play it.

## 10. CAKE, "I Will Survive" (1996)

CAKE's deadpan cover of the Gloria Gaynor classic, from *Fashion Nugget*. A disco anthem with a 90s alt-rock shrug.

## How to use 90s songs at a party

The 90s hit hardest in the **middle of the night**, once the room is warmed up. Start with classic rock and 80s hits, bring in the 90s when the dance floor fills, and finish with 2000s alternative and modern bangers so every age in the room gets its moment. (More on that in our [decade-picking guide](/blog/80s-90s-2000s-party-playlist-ideas/).)
