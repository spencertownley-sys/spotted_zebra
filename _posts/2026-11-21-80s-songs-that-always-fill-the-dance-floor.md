**Quick answer:** The 80s songs that fill a dance floor every time we play them are **"You Shook Me All Night Long" (AC/DC), "Love Shack" (The B-52's), "Should I Stay or Should I Go" (The Clash), "Panama" (Van Halen), "Dancing with Myself" (Billy Idol's Generation X)** and **"(You Gotta) Fight for Your Right (To Party!)" (Beastie Boys)**. Add "She Sells Sanctuary," "I Ran," "Keep Your Hands to Yourself" and "Tainted Love" for a full set.

Like our [90s list](/blog/90s-songs-that-always-fill-the-dance-floor/), these come straight from [our actual set](/jukebox/). They're the ones that get people out of their chairs.

## 1. AC/DC, "You Shook Me All Night Long" (1980)

From *Back in Black*. If you only play one 80s rock song at a party, make it this one. [Watch us play it.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s-XQlZWeMeM)

## 2. The B-52's, "Love Shack" (1989)

From *Cosmic Thing*. Pure party, and everyone knows the "tin roof, rusted" line.

## 3. The Clash, "Should I Stay or Should I Go" (1982)

From *Combat Rock*. That stop-start riff makes people jump on cue. [Watch us play it.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o0kJWJ4sjnw)

## 4. Van Halen, "Panama" (1984)

From *1984*. Big guitar, bigger energy. It's a great way to open a set.

## 5. Generation X, "Dancing with Myself" (1980)

Billy Idol sang it first with Generation X before it became a solo hit. Fast, fun and impossible to stand still to.

## 6. Beastie Boys, "(You Gotta) Fight for Your Right (To Party!)" (1986)

From *Licensed to Ill*. The title says it all. [Watch us play it.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lEYze4neGn4)

## 7. The Cult, "She Sells Sanctuary" (1985)

From *Love*. A swirling guitar intro that builds and builds. [Watch us play it.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8zX0w-uO2iM)

## 8. A Flock of Seagulls, "I Ran (So Far Away)" (1982)

Synth-pop with a guitar edge. The chorus is a guaranteed shout-along.

## 9. The Georgia Satellites, "Keep Your Hands to Yourself" (1986)

A barroom stomp that works in every room we've ever played.

## 10. "Tainted Love" (1981)

Soft Cell made it an 80s classic. We play it in the style of Imelda May's rockabilly version, which gives the song a whole new swing.

## How to use 80s songs at a party

The 80s land best **early in the night**, while the room is warming up. They're the songs every age group knows. Then bring in the [90s](/blog/90s-songs-that-always-fill-the-dance-floor/) as the dance floor fills, and finish with 2000s and modern hits. (More in our [decade-picking guide](/blog/80s-90s-2000s-party-playlist-ideas/).)
